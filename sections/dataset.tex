\section{Datasets, Models, and Metrics}
\label{sec:dataset}

\paragraph{Datasets.} We evaluate wiggle on six datasets, each filtered to items where judges are likely to be uncertain (clear-cut items show little wiggle):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{WildGuard} (safety classification, 100 items): WildGuardMix \citep{wildguardmix2024}, restricted to adversarial prompts with compliant responses.
    \item \textbf{AEGIS} (safety classification, 100 items): a second safety taxonomy \citep{aegis2024} for replication.
    \item \textbf{HH-RLHF} (multi-turn red-team safety, 100 items): Anthropic's red-team-attempts dataset, stratified across harm levels 0--4.
    \item \textbf{ToxiGen} (toxicity detection, 100 items): adversarial toxicity items balanced across demographic targets.
    \item \textbf{MAGE} (AI-generated text detection, 100 items): each item is either human-written or AI-generated, with known provenance.
    \item \textbf{Paired Prompts} (political content, 50 prompt pairs per rubric): prompts are sampled from a non-anchored set, with two independent rubrics --- \emph{hedging} and \emph{refusal}.
\end{itemize}

Five of the six datasets ship with ground-truth labels, enabling per-wiggle classification as \emph{corrective} (toward the label) or \emph{corrupting} (away). Paired Prompts has no canonical ground truth, so it is excluded from corrective/corrupting analyses.

\paragraph{Models.} We evaluate 9 judge models across four families: GPT-5, GPT-5.2, GPT-5.4 (OpenAI); Claude 4.6 Sonnet, Claude 4.6 Opus (Anthropic); Grok-4.1, Grok-4.1 Reasoning (xAI); Gemini 3 Flash, Gemini 3.1 Pro (Google). For L6 adaptive persuasion, three of these (GPT-5.4, Claude Opus, Grok-4.1 Reasoning) serve as persuaders, generating challenges for all judges including, in some cases, themselves. We selected the most recent frontier model available from each of three different organizations (OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI) to ensure variety in the persuader pool and to avoid conflating persuader effects with the idiosyncrasies of any single training family. The L2--L4 counterarguments and L4 consensus sets are pre-generated by the same three persuader models; an observer model (GPT-5) extracts the judge's verdict from free-form responses. Full details in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-pressure-gen}.

\paragraph{Wiggle rate and retention rate.} For each (item, judge, level) triple we compute the wiggle rate $w_\ell(M)$ --- for binary, the flip rate; for Likert, the crossed-sides rate from \S\ref{sec:framework}. The retention rate is $1 - w_\ell(M)$.

\paragraph{Wiggliness and jaggedness.} Let $\bar{w}_c(M)$ denote the mean wiggle rate of judge $M$ on (domain, scale) combination $c$, averaged across all pressure levels. \emph{Wiggliness} is the average of the per-cell means and \emph{jaggedness} is their population variance:
\begin{align}
\mathrm{Wiggliness}(M) &= \frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|} \sum_{c \in \mathcal{C}} \bar{w}_c(M),
\label{eq:wiggliness} \\
\mathrm{Jaggedness}(M) &= \frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|} \sum_{c \in \mathcal{C}} \big(\bar{w}_c(M) - \mathrm{Wiggliness}(M)\big)^2.
\label{eq:jaggedness}
\end{align}
Wiggliness captures overall susceptibility to pressure; jaggedness captures how unevenly susceptibility is distributed across the grid.

\paragraph{Jury baseline.} We additionally compute a domain-general difficulty proxy by having all 9 judges rate each item at L0 (baseline, temp=0, no pressure). The resulting jury majority strength is included as a feature in the cross-level correlation analysis (\S\ref{sec:discussion}).
