\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced the Wiggle Framework, a three-axis diagnostic for measuring LLM-as-judge reliability: Repeatability (stability under silence), Conviction (stability under pressure), and Invariance (stability under framing changes). Applied to safety judgment tasks across multiple frontier models, we demonstrated that these axes capture genuinely independent failure modes, that conviction degradation curves serve as calibration diagnostics, and that persistence survival analysis disambiguates sycophancy from rational updating.

The framework equips practitioners with actionable tools: degradation curves reveal whether a judge is sycophantic, stubborn, or well-calibrated; directional asymmetry analysis identifies which direction a safety gate is vulnerable; and the correct-vs-wrong-label persistence comparison provides a principled sycophancy litmus test.

As LLM-as-judge systems become load-bearing infrastructure for safety evaluation, content moderation, and model launch decisions, the question is no longer \emph{how accurate is this judge?} but \emph{how much does this judge wiggle, and in what ways?}
