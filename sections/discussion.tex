The paper's deepest risk is F2: the degradation curves are the centerpiece, and their value depends on difficulty separation. Everything else is recoverable through reframing. This is why Experiment~1 runs first---if we do not see meaningful variation there, we pivot early.

\subsection{Experiments We Are Not Running}

Several natural extensions were considered and deliberately excluded:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Full 2D conviction landscape} (intensity $\times$ persistence grid): Too expensive---5 pressure levels $\times$ 20 persistence turns $\times$ 5 models $\times$ 500+ items. The 1D slices (Experiments~2 and~3) capture the key phenomena.
    \item \textbf{Cross-domain generalization} (SimpleQA, GSM8K): Would dilute the safety focus. Discussed as future work in Section~\ref{sec:future_work} with testable predictions.
    \item \textbf{Infrastructure decomposition} (temp=0 repeatability analysis): Already covered by the Thinking Machines blog. We include temp=0 runs as a baseline in Experiment~1.
    \item \textbf{Linear probe / logprob confidence}: Requires model internals access unavailable for all models. Verbalized confidence is noisier but universally applicable.
    \item \textbf{Evaluation awareness as primary contribution}: Moved to Experiment~5 as optional to avoid competing with the growing alignment-faking literature.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Practical Recommendations}

Based on our findings, we offer the following guidance for practitioners deploying LLM-as-judge systems:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Measure all three axes.} A judge that repeats itself reliably can still be easily persuaded; a judge that resists persuasion can still be sensitive to framing.
    \item \textbf{Use degradation curves for calibration diagnostics.} The shape of the curve---not just the final accuracy---reveals whether the judge is sycophantic (steep everywhere), stubborn (flat everywhere), or well-calibrated (difficulty-dependent).
    \item \textbf{Check directional asymmetry for safety gates.} A judge that is more easily persuaded toward permissiveness is a greater safety risk than one biased toward restriction.
    \item \textbf{Use persistence analysis to distinguish sycophancy from rational updating.} The correct-vs-wrong-label comparison is the critical diagnostic.
\end{enumerate}
